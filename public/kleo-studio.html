Kleo Studio – Hair Salon Brand & Packaging

UI/UX Design Project 2 (EGL312) · Nanyang Polytechnic · 2023 · Team project

My part
The product line: labels, colour variants and packaging
Products
Shampoo, conditioner, hair mask, serum, hair oil, hair spray
Tools
Adobe Illustrator (labels), Adobe Dimension (3D mockups), Photoshop
Team deliverables
Persona, logo, tote bag, flyer, loyalty card, Instagram stories

The brand

Kleo Studio is a neighbourhood hair salon. Our team built a calm, premium identity that would make customers feel the salon understands their hair, and extend it into a line of the salon’s own hair care products. My part was that product line.

My part: the product line

Exploring label directions

Before settling on a design, I explored four directions for the Tea Tree range, testing how much of the label the logo, product type and benefits should take up.

Direction 4 became the final design. Left-aligned text reads faster on a curved bottle, moving the logo to the corner gives the product name room, and the star accent ties the label back to the stars around the salon’s emblem. The leaf background looked natural, but it made the text harder to read and wouldn’t work across every scent.

The final labels

Every label follows the same layout: product type in a tag at the top, scent name as the hero, then hair type, benefit and size, with the emblem in the corner. Only the scent colour changes (green for Tea Tree, purple for Lavender), so a customer can tell the range apart on the shelf while it stays recognisably Kleo. Proofreading mattered here: the early drafts read “oliy hair”, which I caught and corrected before the final labels.

Packaging in 3D

I applied the labels to 3D bottle and jar models in Adobe Dimension, checking how the layout wrapped around curved surfaces before exporting the final mockups.

What I learned

  • Exploring several directions before committing made the final choice easy to justify: each draft answered a question about layout and readability.
  • A label is a system, not a one-off. Fixing the layout and changing only the scent colour let one design scale across six products.
  • Small text errors survive many drafts. Reading the labels as a customer would, not as the designer, is what caught “oliy”.
  • Designing flat and checking in 3D catches problems a flat artboard hides, such as text wrapping around a curved bottle.
